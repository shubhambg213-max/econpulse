Weekly Wrap-up
Week of 30 August – 5 September 2026
🔎 The week in one line: A dovish start flipped hawkish on Friday. Indian equities snapped a four-session slide on 4 Sep — Sensex +363 to 76,515, Nifty at 23,898, metals-led — closing just before a hot US August jobs report (162k vs ~55k expected, jobless rate 4.1%) reignited September rate-hike fears, drove US 2- and 5-year yields to 52-week highs and the 10-year to 4.78%, and knocked the S&P 500 off its 3-Sep record of 7,748. Oil re-escalated: Brent jumped back to ~$97 as Kuwait repelled Iranian missile-and-drone strikes and fresh US-Iran clashes revived Strait-of-Hormuz supply fears, with throughput near a fifth of pre-war levels. At home the growth story held — Q1 FY27 GDP's 7.8% beat and August GST up 14.8% to ₹1.99 lakh crore — but a 14% monsoon rain deficit now threatens rice and corn and pushed food inflation to a 2026-high 5.5% in July, sharpening the RBI's food-price dilemma even as it holds repo at 5.25%. The rupee (₹94.5) and the 10-year G-sec (~6.96%) head into a jittery new week under renewed pressure; the IMF still pegs 2026 global growth at 3.0%.
Monthly Wrap-up
Past 30 days · to 5 September 2026
🔎 The month in one line: A growth surprise wrapped in an oil shock, ending with a hawkish jolt. India's Q1 FY27 GDP, out 31 Aug, printed a robust 7.8% — beating the RBI's 7% estimate on 9.2% manufacturing and 12.1% financial-real-estate-IT services, though decelerating from 8.6% as farm growth eased to 3.6%; August GST climbed 14.8% to ₹1.99 lakh crore and bank credit ran near a two-year-high 18.6%, even as the Q1 current-account deficit widened to $4.2bn (0.5% of GDP). CPI re-accelerated to a 19-month-high 4.45% (July) and a 14% monsoon rain deficit now threatens rice and corn — pushing food inflation to a 2026-high 5.5% and prompting duty-free sugar imports; the RBI held repo at 5.25% a fourth time on 5 Aug. Q1 earnings beat a feared decline as BFSI (+20%) and metals (+53%) led. Crude drove the swings — Brent dipped below $90, then spiked back to ~$97 by early September as US-Iran clashes (including strikes near Kuwait) revived Hormuz fears. The month's sting came last: after an early-week soft-ADP dovish dip, a hot August US payrolls print (162k vs ~55k expected) on 4 Sep flipped the debate toward a September hike, driving US 2- and 5-year yields to 52-week highs and the 10-year to 4.78%, knocking the S&P 500 off its 3-Sep record 7,748 and leaving the rupee (₹94.5) and 10-year G-sec (~6.96%) under renewed pressure. The IMF kept global growth at 3.0%, crediting AI investment for offsetting war-and-trade headwinds.
